# Original Interview Excerpt — Olivia Ramirez, Harbor & Finch

**Interviewer:** Laura Bennett  
**Customer:** Olivia Ramirez, Operations Manager at Harbor & Finch  
**Topic:** Inventory tracking and Smart Inventory Alerts

## Excerpt

**Laura:** How are you tracking inventory today?

**Olivia:** We export from Shopify every Monday and compare it against the warehouse report. Then my inventory coordinator updates a spreadsheet with what we think needs attention. It works, but it is very manual.

**Laura:** What usually goes wrong?

**Olivia:** We usually catch low-stock issues when someone notices the spreadsheet is off or when customer service starts asking why a product is unavailable. The worst time is before a seasonal promotion because the reorder timing gets tight.

**Laura:** Would automated low-stock alerts help?

**Olivia:** Maybe. I like the idea, but only if the alert is actually based on current data. If it is just another notification, people will ignore it. We already get too many alerts from Shopify apps.

**Laura:** What would make an alert trustworthy?

**Olivia:** It would need to show why the item is being flagged. For example: current stock, sales velocity, supplier lead time, and whether a promotion is coming. I would also want to set my own thresholds for certain product lines.

**Laura:** Would you want reorder suggestions too?

**Olivia:** Eventually, maybe. But I would start with alerts. I do not want the system telling us to reorder unless I can see the logic. Our finance person still wants approval before we commit to a purchase.

**Laura:** What should the product team test next?

**Olivia:** I would test whether the alert is specific enough to be useful. If it says “low stock,” that is not enough. If it says “this will likely stock out before the Memorial Day promotion,” that is useful.
