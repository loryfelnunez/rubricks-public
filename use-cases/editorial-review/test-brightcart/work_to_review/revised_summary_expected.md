# Revised Customer Interview Summary — Expected Direction

## Executive Summary

Olivia Ramirez, Operations Manager at Harbor & Finch, described inventory tracking as manual and timing-sensitive. Her team exports Shopify data, compares it against warehouse reports, and updates a shared spreadsheet to decide which products need attention.

She expressed interest in Smart Inventory Alerts, but with important caveats. Alerts would need to be accurate, explain why a product is being flagged, and avoid adding to existing notification fatigue.

**Confidence:** Medium. This interview supports further validation of configurable inventory alerts, but it does not support broad demand or immediate roadmap commitment on its own.

## Evidence-Backed Pain Points

- Inventory review is manual and depends on weekly Shopify exports, warehouse reports, and spreadsheet updates.
- Low-stock issues are sometimes caught late, especially before seasonal promotions.
- The customer wants alerts that explain the reason for the flag, including stock level, sales velocity, supplier lead time, and promotion context.

## Objections and Constraints

- The customer said people may ignore alerts if they are not based on current data.
- The customer already receives too many notifications from Shopify apps.
- Reorder suggestions require more trust and finance approval before purchase decisions.

## Recommended Next Steps

- Validate whether users prefer low-stock alerts, stockout-risk alerts, or reorder suggestions.
- Test alert explanations with 3–5 similar operations users.
- Include alert logic in the prototype: current stock, sales velocity, supplier lead time, and promotion timing.
- Do not prioritize automated reorder recommendations until trust and approval requirements are better understood.
