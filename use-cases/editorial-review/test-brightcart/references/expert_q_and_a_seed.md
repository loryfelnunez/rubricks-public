# Expert Q&A Seed — Customer Interview Summary Review

**Expert:** Laura Bennett, Independent Product & Operations Consultant  
**Rubrick:** Customer Interview Summary Review  
**Use case:** Review AI-generated summaries of customer discovery interviews before they are shared with product, design, sales, or client leadership.

## Guided Q&A

### 1. What makes a customer interview summary useful?
A useful customer interview summary helps the reader understand what the customer actually said, what the reviewer inferred, and what still needs validation. It should include the customer’s role, current workflow, pain points, objections, evidence, and recommended next steps.

### 2. What should a summary never do?
It should not turn one customer’s comment into broad market demand. It should not make the customer sound more positive than they were. It should not hide objections, skip uncertainty, or recommend product work without evidence.

### 3. What details must be included?
A good summary should include:
- Customer role and company context
- Current workflow or process
- Specific pain points
- Direct quotes or transcript-backed evidence
- Objections, hesitation, or constraints
- Recommended next steps
- Confidence level or limitation when evidence is thin

### 4. What common mistakes do AI-generated summaries make?
AI summaries often sound polished but flatten nuance. They overstate enthusiasm, remove hesitation, soften negative feedback, and make recommendations that feel more certain than the source supports.

### 5. When should the reviewer request changes?
Request changes when the summary:
- Makes unsupported claims
- Omits objections or caveats
- Lacks evidence or quotes
- Confuses facts with interpretation
- Recommends a roadmap action without validation
- Has vague next steps

### 6. What should Rubricks do when a summary is weak?
Rubricks should explain what is missing, identify the matched guidance, suggest a more accurate edit, and recommend any follow-up question or work item needed before the summary is shared.
