# Summary Quality Standard

A customer interview summary is considered ready when it is accurate, evidence-backed, balanced, and decision-useful.

## Required Elements

### 1. Customer context
The summary should identify who the customer is, their role, company type, and relevant operating environment.

### 2. Current workflow
The summary should describe how the customer currently performs the process being discussed.

### 3. Pain points
The summary should identify specific problems, not generic dissatisfaction.

### 4. Evidence
Important claims should be supported by direct quotes, paraphrased source evidence, or clear references to the interview.

### 5. Objections and constraints
The summary should preserve hesitation, blockers, budget concerns, process constraints, or trust issues.

### 6. Interpretation boundaries
The summary should distinguish between:
- What the customer said
- What the reviewer inferred
- What the product team might do next

### 7. Actionable next steps
Next steps should be specific enough to act on. Where possible, they should identify the type of follow-up, validation need, or decision relevance.

### 8. Confidence level
If the conclusion is based on limited evidence, the summary should say so.

## Not Ready Indicators

A summary is not ready if it:
- Overstates demand
- Omits key objections
- Lacks evidence
- Makes unsupported recommendations
- Uses vague next steps
- Hides uncertainty
- Turns one interview into a broad conclusion
